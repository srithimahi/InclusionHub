# InclusionHub

An accessibility-focused project built by **Srithika Mahendran and Vidhika Neema**, exploring how technology can make communication and everyday interactions more accessible.

InclusionHub combines accessibility tools centered around audio and speech, with each of us taking ownership of different parts of the project.

---

## About the Project

InclusionHub started from the idea that everyday communication can become a barrier when information is primarily delivered through sound or speech.

We wanted to experiment with tools that could translate those forms of information into something more accessible.

The project includes two major areas we worked on:

- **Speech transcription** — developed by Srithika
- **Sound alerts** — developed by Vidhika

Rather than treating accessibility as an extra feature added onto an existing application, InclusionHub was built around accessibility from the beginning.

---

## Our Contributions

### Srithika Mahendran — Transcription

I worked primarily on the **transcription side of InclusionHub**.

My part of the project focused on taking spoken audio and converting it into readable text, allowing information communicated verbally to also be accessed visually.

This involved working with the transcription pipeline and integrating it into the application.

### Vidhika Neema — Sound Alerts

Vidhika worked primarily on the **sound-alert system**.

Her portion of the project focused on detecting or responding to important sounds and providing an alternative alert to the user.

Together, the two systems explore different sides of the same accessibility problem: making audio-based information available in other forms.

---

## Why We Built It

A lot of technology assumes that users receive information in the same way.

But something as ordinary as an announcement, conversation, alarm, or other sound can become inaccessible when audio is the only way that information is communicated.

InclusionHub was our attempt to explore what happens when accessibility is part of the original problem rather than something considered afterward.
